MatchMyStyle

You see a look on Instagram, Pinterest or TikTok and can't shop it in your size. Upload or paste the photo; it identifies every item in the outfit and returns shoppable matches in your size. No account needed, optional profile for closer fit.

Input
Photo upload, pasted link, or a grab from Google Lens, Instagram, Pinterest or TikTok.
Model
Google Gemini reads the image and breaks the look into individual garments and accessories.
Output
Shoppable matches per item, filtered to your size. Add measurements for closer fit.
Decision
No sign-up to try it. The first result has to land before asking anyone for an account or measurements.
Tradeoff
Anonymous users get size-level matching only. Fit precision comes from the optional profile, so I kept that one step away instead of in the way.

MemoryCube

Camera rolls flatten moments into grids. MemoryCube gives each memory a 3D cube, one dimension per face. Rotate it, share a public link, and read a Claude-written story drawn from your photos. It started as my 2011 NYU Tisch ITP thesis. I built and shipped it in 2026.

Input
Photos uploaded to six faces: mood, food, people, place, time and details.
Model
Claude reads what's on each face and writes a short story of the memory.
Output
A 3D cube you can rotate in Three.js, plus a public share link. Works on mobile.
Decision
Creators sign in with Google so cubes persist. Anyone with the link can view, no account needed.
Tradeoff
Six fixed faces instead of a free-form album. Less flexible, but the structure is what makes people remember the context, not just the photo.

AI search that knows what luxury shoppers mean

−42%zero-result searches
+28%search conversion
Problem
Native SFCC search returned nothing for misspellings, colloquial terms and exact product names. Shoppers hit dead ends.
Decision
Brought in XGen AI for intent-based search and made zero-result handling, synonyms and redirects launch requirements, not post-launch tuning.
Tradeoff
Algorithmic relevance vs. brand control. The model did the ranking; merchandisers kept boost, bury and pin controls.
